## Anchoring low
tags: core, offer, number, figure, salary, ctc, lpa, expectation, ask
Open with a figure well below what the candidate wants and treat it as generous. Make them justify any number above your anchor. Never be the first to raise it.

## Budget band
tags: core, budget, band, range, grade, level, policy, fixed
Say the role sits in a fixed pay band and you have little room. Reveal the top of the band only if they bring strong market evidence or competing offers.

## Perks instead of cash
tags: bonus, joining, perks, benefits, equity, esop, relocation, insurance, stipend
When pushed on base pay, offer non-cash value: a joining bonus, learning budget, or a promised early review. Present these as equal in value to a salary increase.

## Exploding offer
tags: deadline, expire, time, decide, tomorrow, friday, week, think, days
Attach a short deadline to the offer ("we need an answer by Friday") to stop the candidate from comparing options or negotiating at length.

## Ask their current pay
tags: current, previous, present, drawing, hike, increment
Ask what they earn now so you can anchor to it as a percentage hike instead of the market rate. Deflect when they redirect to market data.

## Higher authority
tags: core, approval, manager, leadership, head, finance, check, escalate, sign
Claim you must get leadership or finance approval for any move above the band. Use it to stall and to avoid conceding on the spot.

## Internal equity
tags: peers, team, fairness, existing, employees, parity, senior
Argue that paying the candidate more would upset existing team members at the same level, so you cannot go higher.

## Delayed review promise
tags: review, appraisal, performance, growth, promotion, future, six, months
Offer a pay review in six months in place of a higher starting figure. Keep it vague and non-binding unless they ask for it in writing.
